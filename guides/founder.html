the six-critic prompt

hey, it's KP. you commented FOUNDER, so here it is. here's the six-critic prompt from the reel, the follow-up i run after it, and how to read the results without talking yourself out of a good idea.

the prompt

Act as 6 critics reviewing my business idea:
1. a skeptical investor
2. my target customer
3. a competitor
4. a CFO
5. a lawyer
6. a burned-out founder who tried this before

Each one gives me the single biggest reason this fails, in 2 sentences.
Then tell me which critique is the most dangerous and what I should test this week to answer it.

My idea: [idea, who it's for, how it makes money, what I've done so far]

the follow-up

Take the most dangerous critique. Design the smallest test I can run in 7 days for under $[budget] that would prove it wrong or right.
Give me: the test, what result means "keep going", what result means "change the idea", and the exact message or post I'd use to run it.

how to read it

  • the customer critic matters most. investors and lawyers can be answered later. if the customer critic is right, nothing else matters.
  • look for repeats. if three critics say the same thing in different words, that's the real problem.
  • a critique isn't a verdict. the point is a test, not a reason to quit. run the test, then decide.

that's Off-Script Rich: kill the idea on paper so it doesn't kill your savings.

good to know

  • give it real detail. "an app for creators" gets generic critics. who it's for, the price and what you've already tried gets useful ones.
  • claude can be too nice or too harsh. run it twice in fresh chats and compare. the critiques that show up both times are the real ones.
  • the lawyer critic isn't a lawyer. if it flags a legal risk, check it with an actual one.
