19 out of 19 were higher. the average was about +15.1% and the median +14.5%.

what the green hides

this is the part that matters if you actually hold through it. in 11 of those 19 years, the index fell more than 10% from a peak at some point during the following 12 months. in 1986 it fell 33.2% along the way and still finished the year 1.1% higher.

so the real lesson isn't "midterms are bullish". it's that the people who got the green number were the ones who didn't sell during the red part.

why the streak might exist

the usual explanation is that the uncertainty ends. before a midterm nobody knows who controls congress. after it, markets can price the next two years, and a split government usually means fewer big surprises from washington. that's a story that fits the data. it isn't proof.

that's Off-Script Rich: read the history before the headline.

Do not predict 2027 returns.

good to know

  • 19 is a small sample. the source itself says the pattern isn't statistically proven. rates and earnings were doing most of the work in every one of those years.
  • price only. these are price returns, without dividends. other sources quote different averages because they use different start years and windows.
  • past returns aren't a promise. the 2026 row is a question mark on purpose.
