the red-day checklist

hey, it's KP. you commented RED, so here it is. this is the checklist from the reel, the one i run before i touch anything on a red day. it takes about ten minutes and it's built to stop me from selling on a feeling.

who this is for

you, if you just watched your account drop and your thumb is hovering over sell. skip it if you're a day trader, because this is built for people who hold for years.

the checklist

RED DAY CHECKLIST              date: ______

1. what's down?          ticker/fund: ______  -__% today
2. why?                  one line, from a real source: ______
3. did the BUSINESS change, or only the PRICE?
                         business / price
4. would i buy it today at this price if i didn't own it?
                         yes / no / not sure
5. do i need this money in the next 12 months?
                         yes / no

if 3 = price and 5 = no  -> do nothing. close the app.
if 3 = business          -> re-read why you bought it.
if 5 = yes               -> that money was in the wrong place.
                            fix the plan, not today's trade.

i fill this in after the close at 4pm eastern, never during the drop. the market will still be there tomorrow.

before you sell at a loss: the wash sale rule

if you sell something at a loss and buy the same or a substantially identical security within 30 days before or after the sale, the IRS calls it a wash sale and you can't deduct that loss now. instead it gets added to the cost basis of the new shares. so selling to "harvest" a loss and buying straight back doesn't do what people think.

this only matters in a taxable brokerage account. inside a 401k or IRA, gains and losses aren't taxed year to year anyway.

what i actually do on red days

  • keep the automatic contributions running. buying on schedule means red days buy more shares for the same money.
  • write the number down, then stop looking. checking every hour doesn't change the price.
  • keep the emergency fund separate. people sell on red days when they need the cash. if the cash lives elsewhere, the red day is just a number.

that's Off-Script Rich: staying in the game on the red days too.

the prompt

My [ticker or fund] is down [x]% today.
1. Find the main reason reported today and give me the source.
2. Did anything about the business itself change (earnings, guidance, a lawsuit, a product) or is this a market-wide or sector move?
3. List 3 questions I should answer before deciding to do anything.
Do not tell me to buy or sell, and do not predict tomorrow's price.

good to know

  • not a reason to never sell. if question 3 says the business really changed, selling can be the right call. the checklist just makes sure it's the business deciding and not your stomach.
  • wash sales count across accounts. buying the same stock in your IRA, or your spouse buying it, can still trigger the rule. ask a tax pro if you're harvesting losses.
  • a heatmap helps with question 2. comment MAP on that reel for the two-minute read.

proof

  • IRS Publication 550, Investment Income and Expenses · see the Wash Sales section
  • Fidelity: wash-sale rules · 30 days before or after, disallowed loss added to the cost basis of the new shares
