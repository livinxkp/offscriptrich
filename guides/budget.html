hey, it's KP. you commented BUDGET, so here it is. here's the two-column budget from the reel, how i got to the $5,300 take-home number, where the $1,500 a month gap actually comes from, and a calculator that gives every dollar of your own paycheck a job.

how $85k becomes about $5,300 a month

rough estimate for a single filer in california earning $85,000 in 2026, taking the standard deduction, with no pre-tax 401k contributions:

lineper year
gross pay$85,000
federal income tax (2026 brackets, $16,100 standard deduction)about $9,870
social security and medicare (7.65%)about $6,500
california income taxabout $3,660
california SDI (1.3% in 2026)about $1,100
take-homeabout $63,850, or $5,320 a month

your real number depends on your 401k contributions, health insurance and anything else your employer takes out. use your pay stub, not this table.

same $5,300. the gap is $1,500 a month in the invest line, which is $18,000 a year. the left side isn't reckless. it's money with no job, so it finds one on its own.

where the $1,500 actually came from

  • rent: $650 a month, from a roommate.
  • the car: $600 a month, from a paid-off car.
  • everything else: $250 a month, from smaller lines added up.

the two biggest lines did most of the work. cutting coffee wouldn't have got anywhere close. that's Off-Script Rich: fix the big lines and let the small ones breathe.

good to know

  • the tax line is an estimate. california brackets for 2026 are adjusted for inflation each year. i used the latest published schedule, so treat the result as within a few dollars a month, not exact.
  • a roommate isn't for everyone. it's just the biggest single lever in this example. your biggest lever might be different.
  • start with the invest line, not the cuts. set the transfer on payday and spend what's left.

proof

  • IRS: tax inflation adjustments for tax year 2026 · single standard deduction $16,100, 12% bracket to $50,400, 22% to $105,700
