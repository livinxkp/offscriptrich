dip or trap: the two-question test

hey, it's KP. you commented DIP, so here it is. this is the two-question test from the reel, where to find both numbers for free in about five minutes, and the prompt that pulls them out of a filing for you.

who this is for

you, if you buy individual stocks and the word "dip" makes you want to hit buy. skip it if you only own index funds, because an index doesn't need this test.

the test

  1. is revenue still growing? compare the latest quarter's revenue to the same quarter a year ago. same quarter matters, because a lot of businesses are seasonal.
  2. is it making more cash than it spends? look at free cash flow: cash from operations minus capital expenditures. positive means the business funds itself. negative means it needs outside money to keep going.

two yeses: the business didn't break, the price did. that's a dip worth looking at. two nos: the price is falling because the business is. that's a trap. one of each: dig further before you do anything.

where to find both numbers

  1. go to SEC EDGAR and search the company.
  2. open the latest 10-Q (quarterly report) or 10-K (annual report).
  3. revenue is on the first line of the income statement. it might say "revenue", "net sales" or "total revenues".
  4. in the cash flow statement, find "net cash provided by operating activities" and "purchases of property and equipment" (that's capex). subtract the second from the first.

or use the company's investor relations page, which posts the same numbers in an earnings release.

the worksheet

TICKER: ______      PRICE DOWN __% THIS MONTH

1. revenue this quarter:         $______
   revenue same quarter last yr: $______
   growing?                       YES / NO

2. operating cash flow (last 12 months): $______
   minus capex (last 12 months):         $______
   = free cash flow:                     $______
   positive?                              YES / NO

two yeses = dip      two nos = trap      one each = dig more

that's Off-Script Rich: check the business before you check the chart.

the prompt

Read [company]'s most recent 10-Q and the 10-Q from the same quarter last year.
1. Revenue this quarter vs the same quarter last year, with the % change. Quote the line and page.
2. Operating cash flow and capital expenditures for the last 12 months, and free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capex).
3. One sentence: did the business change, or only the price?
If a number isn't in the filing, say "not in the filing". Do not tell me whether to buy.

good to know

  • two questions isn't a full analysis. it's a filter. debt, valuation and competition all matter too. the test just stops the most common mistake, buying a business that's shrinking.
  • young companies often fail question two on purpose. a fast-growing company can burn cash while it builds. that's why one-of-each means dig more, not walk away.
  • ai misreads filings. the prompt asks for the line and page. check the two numbers your decision depends on.

proof

  • SEC EDGAR full-text search · every 10-Q and 10-K, free
  • Investor.gov (SEC): using EDGAR to research investments · how to find a company's filings
