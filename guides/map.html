the two-minute heatmap read

hey, it's KP. you commented MAP, so here it is. the reel was one picture of a red market. this is how i read that picture every morning in about two minutes, plus the prompt that explains a screenshot back to you in plain words.

who this is for

you, if you own an index fund or a few stocks and you want to know what actually happened today without watching a single talking head. skip it if you want a trade signal. a heatmap tells you what moved. it doesn't tell you what to do.

what you're looking at

open finviz.com/map.ashx, the free s&p 500 map. every box is one company. two things are encoded in it.

  • size is market cap. the bigger the company, the bigger the box. that's why a handful of tech giants take up a huge chunk of the screen.
  • colour is the % move. green is up, red is down, and the deeper the colour the bigger the move. grey is roughly flat.

boxes are grouped by sector, so you can see in one glance whether it's one industry bleeding or the whole board.

the two-minute routine

  1. step back first. is the whole map one colour, or is it patchy? one colour means a broad move, usually rates, a macro number or an election. patchy means stock or sector news.
  2. find the biggest red box. because boxes are sized by market cap, a big red box moves the index more than ten small ones. that's the one to read about.
  3. find the odd one out. one green box in a red sector (or the other way round) is almost always earnings or company news.
  4. check the sector you own most. if your index fund is heavy in tech, the tech block is your day. everything else is background.

then close it. checking it ten times a day doesn't change what you own.

the prompt

screenshot the map and paste it into chatgpt or claude with this:

This is a screenshot of today's S&P 500 heatmap. Box size is market cap, colour is today's % move.
1. In one sentence: was today a broad move or a sector/stock move?
2. Name the 3 largest boxes that are red and their approximate % move as shown.
3. Name any stock moving against its sector.
4. Which sector had the worst day by colour?
Only describe what is visible in the image. If a number isn't readable, say "not readable" instead of guessing. Do not tell me what to buy or sell.

that's Off-Script Rich: read the market before the market reads you.

good to know

  • the colour isn't the index. a map can look mostly red while the s&p is only slightly down, because a few giant boxes carry most of the weight. check the actual index number before you repeat a headline.
  • check the timestamp. free maps can lag the live market. if you're reading it mid-session, compare one box against your broker's live price.
  • ai reads pictures badly at small sizes. zoom into the sector you care about before you screenshot, or it will invent numbers. the prompt tells it not to, and it still sometimes does.
  • a red day is information, not an instruction. if you want the checklist i run before touching anything on a red day, comment RED on that reel.

proof

  • Finviz: S&P 500 map · free, box size = market cap, colour = performance
  • Finviz blog: new stock market maps · the other map views (market cap, 52-week highs and lows, themes)
