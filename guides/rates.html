what to actually charge brands

hey, it's KP. you commented RATES, so here it is. the reel was me pricing one deal out loud. this is the whole thing — the base number, the four multipliers that actually move it, what the 2026 benchmark data says brands really pay, and a rate card you can copy.

who this is for

you, if a brand has landed in your inbox and you genuinely don't know what number to say back. this works from your first deal up to roughly the 500k mark. skip it if you've already got a manager negotiating for you, or if what's on the table is a long-term ambassador retainer — that's a different structure with different math.

why this matters

the first number you say out loud becomes your record. brands keep notes, agencies share rate sheets, and the next company to email you often already knows what you took last time. you are not pricing one deal. you are setting a floor you'll have to live above for a year.

and almost everyone anchors to the wrong thing. the tier charts that circulate on Instagram describe a slice of the market most creators will never be in. anchor there and you price yourself out of every real offer. anchor low and you work for product. the fix is knowing both numbers — what the market clears at, and what the ceiling actually is.

what the market actually pays

start here, because this is the number nobody posts.

Collabstr's 2026 report analysed more than 21,000 collaborations across over 200,000 creators. the average posted Instagram rate was $214. the average amount brands actually paid was $193. TikTok ran $182 asked against $186 paid. UGC ran $180 asked against $154 paid. YouTube was the outlier at $311 asked, $255 paid.

the distribution is the real story: 80% of all collaborations came in under $300, 18% between $301 and $1,000, and only 2% above $1,000. niche moves it — entrepreneur and business averaged $295, education $307, skilled trades $309, beauty $210.

now hold that against the published tier chart: Hootsuite puts Instagram micro creators at 10k–50k followers around $200–$2,000 a post, and mid-tier 50k–500k at $2,000–$5,000. both sets of numbers are true. the tier chart is what a brand with a real media budget pays a creator with representation. the $193 is what the open marketplace clears at. know which room you're negotiating in before you open your mouth.

the base number

you need one number you can defend, not a feeling.

there are two ways to get it. run both, take the higher, and be able to explain either one on a call.

the follower method is the rule of thumb Hootsuite cites: $100 per 10,000 followers as a working base. it's fast and every brand recognises it. the view method is better, and it's what you use if your reach outruns your follower count: Influencer Marketing Hub puts influencer CPM in the $5 to $25 range depending on audience quality and geography.

use views wherever you can. Socialinsider studied 35 million Instagram posts across 447,613 pages and found the platform's average engagement rate sat at 0.45% in Q2 2026, down 24% year over year, with Reels at 0.48%. followers are a decaying proxy. views are what the brand is buying.

BASE RATE — run both, take the higher

A. follower method
   base = (followers / 10,000) x $100

B. view method
   base = (median views of your last 9 reels / 1,000) x CPM
   CPM: $5 low  |  $12 mid  |  $25 strong niche + strong audience

C. sanity check
   if A and B are more than 3x apart, B is right and your
   follower count is lying about your reach. lead with views.

your base = $__________  (this is ONE organic post, no rights,
                          no exclusivity, no ads. nothing else.)

the four multipliers

the base is the smallest number in the deal. everything real gets added on top.

this is where creators leave the most money, because they quote a base and then hand over rights for free. published ranges, so you can point at them in an email:

  • usage rights — the brand reposting your content on their own channels or in ads. Influee puts this at 30–50% of base. InfluencerFee puts rights and whitelisting together at +80–150% of the base creative fee.
  • whitelisting and paid amplification — ads running from your handle, or their post boosted behind paid spend. InfluencerFee's benchmark is a 25–50% uplift as standard, rising to 50–100% for a 30-day Instagram whitelist and 30–60% for a 30-day TikTok Spark Ads window. Influee prices it at roughly 30% of base per month.
  • exclusivity — you agreeing not to work with their competitors. InfluencerFee benchmarks it at a 1.5–2x multiplier. i think percentage multipliers are the lazy way to price this and there's better math, which is the DEAL guide.
  • deliverable count — Influee's bundle discounts run 5–10% for two pieces, 10–15% for three or four, and around 19% at five or more. never apply a bundle discount in the same deal where rights are expanding. more content plus more rights is not a volume purchase, it's two asks.
THE STACK — build the number in this order

1  base (one organic post)                     $______
2  + usage rights        (+30-50% of base)     $______
3  + paid amplification  (+25-100% of base)    $______
4  + exclusivity          (see DEAL guide)     $______
5  + extra deliverables   (base x count,
                           discount only if
                           rights stay flat)   $______

format math, so you're not underpricing a reel

not every deliverable is worth the same and brands will happily let you forget that.

Influencer Marketing Hub prices Reels at 1.2 to 1.5 times a standard image post, and Stories at 30–50% of the feed post rate. Aspire's benchmark data runs the other direction and says static posts are about 50–60% of the equivalent Reel rate, with carousels 25–33% above static. price the Reel first and derive everything else from it.

Aspire's nano tier is the useful reality check: a median of $250 for an Instagram Reel, with the middle 50% between $150 and $400 and a full range from $10 to nearly $1,000. niche swings it hard — home design Reels benchmarked at $1,500, pet at $1,000, travel at $750, beauty at $225.

and read Aspire's own caveat, because it's the most important sentence in their whole report: those benchmarks "reflect base flat-fee rates only and do not account for content usage rights, exclusivity clauses, or other deal terms." every chart you've screenshotted is the floor. not the price.

running the deal from the reel

here's the exact shape of the offer i was pricing on camera.

a hard tech company wanted an Instagram Reel, a collab post on their feed, and 30 days of boosting. three separate things, and creators routinely charge for one.

the collab post is a second deliverable, not a free repost — it lives on their grid permanently and carries your handle. the 30 days of boosting is paid amplification: they're putting money behind content with your face on it. that is not full whitelisting, which is ads served from your handle as the advertiser, so it prices between a plain organic post and a full whitelist. that gap is exactly where the premium sits.

WORKED EXAMPLE — reel + collab post + 30 days boosting

                                               ------------
true value                                     ~ $B x 1.8 - 2.0

the rate card

copy this, fill it once, and stop rewriting your prices from scratch every email.

a rate card does two jobs. it gets you a number fast, and it quietly tells the brand that rights and exclusivity are line items rather than favours. send it as a PDF once they've told you what they want — never before, because you'll price the small version of the deal.

[YOUR NAME] — RATE CARD [YEAR]
@handle  |  [followers] followers  |  [median reel views] median views

CONTENT
  instagram reel                                 $____
  instagram collab post (with your grid)         $____
  static feed post                               $____
  carousel                                       $____
  story frame (each)                             $____
  UGC video, no posting on my channels           $____

RIGHTS + AMPLIFICATION  (added to content fee)
  organic usage, 30 days                         +30%
  organic usage, 12 months                       +____
  paid amplification / boosting, per 30 days     +30%
  whitelisting from my handle, per 30 days       +50%
  perpetuity / unlimited rights                  +____
  raw footage delivered                          +30%

TERMS
  category exclusivity                           quoted per deal
  50% on signature, 50% within 14 days of delivery
  2 rounds of revisions included, $____ per round after
  all sponsored content disclosed per FTC 16 CFR Part 255
  rates valid 30 days from date of this card

the last two lines do more work than the prices. they say you've done this before.

disclose it, or it's not income, it's exposure

this is the part that's actually law, and it's short.

the FTC's Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255, revised in 2023, require you to disclose what they call a "material connection" to the brand. their own definition: "a personal, family, or employment relationship or a financial relationship – such as the brand paying you or giving you free or discounted products or services." free product counts. gifting counts. you not being asked to say anything specific doesn't get you out of it.

on placement, the FTC is blunt: "the disclosure should be placed with the endorsement message itself. disclosures are likely to be missed if they appear only on an ABOUT ME or profile page, at the end of posts or videos, or anywhere that requires a person to click MORE." for video, if the endorsement is visual the disclosure has to be at least visual; if it's spoken, at least spoken. Stories get an overlay held long enough to read. lives get it repeated for people joining late.

and stay away from "sp", "spon", "collab", or a standalone "thanks" or "ambassador" — the FTC names those specifically as too vague. the brand carries responsibility too, but you're the one whose face is on it. this is the boring line item that keeps everything else you build standing up. that's Off-Script Rich.

DISCLOSURE LINES THAT ACTUALLY CLEAR

caption, first line, before the "more" cut:
  #ad
  #sponsored
  paid partnership with [BRAND]
  thanks to [BRAND] for the free [PRODUCT]

in the video: say it out loud in the first 5 seconds
  "this one's a paid partnership with [BRAND]."
plus on-screen text reading #ad for the same beats.

never use: #sp  #spon  #collab  "thanks"  "ambassador" alone

good to know

  • a brand that says yes to your first number immediately has told you that you were under. that's not a win, that's data — reprice the next one up.
  • product-only deals are the worst version of this. the FTC counts free product as a material connection, so you carry the full disclosure obligation and the full legal exposure with none of the cash.
  • "30 days of boosting" starts when they run the ads, not when you post. brands sit on content for months. put the start date and the end date in writing or that window quietly runs forever.
  • don't reprice off one viral reel. CPM math breaks on outliers — use the median of your last nine, not your best one, or your rate card becomes a number you can't deliver against twice.
