life MINDSET

everyone had a day one

hey, it's KP. you commented MINDSET, so here it is. the reel was about looking at someone at the top and remembering they had a starting point too. this is how i turn that feeling into something i use daily: a mission card, one morning question, and a weekly receipt, so "day one" doesn't stay day one.

who this is for

you, if you're early on something, can see exactly where you want to end up, and the distance sometimes feels discouraging instead of motivating. skip it if you're looking for a quote to save. this one only works if you fill it in.

why comparing up can work for you

looking at someone who's already made it can go two ways. it can make you feel behind, or it can show you the path exists. the difference is usually whether you're comparing your day one to their year fifteen, or using their story as proof that day one is a normal place to start.

everyone you admire was once unknown, unproven and early. the awards and the money are what we see. the years of showing up with nothing to show for it are what we don't. you're in that part now. so is almost everyone.

the mission card

write it once. keep it where you see it in the morning: phone lock screen, mirror, notebook cover.

THE MISSION CARD

what i'm building: ___________________________________

why it matters to me (one line, no one else's reason):
_____________________________________________________

who i'm becoming to build it (three behaviours):
1. __________________________________________________
2. __________________________________________________
3. __________________________________________________

the person who already did something like this, and
what their day one looked like: ______________________

today is day ____.

the morning question

one question, before your phone:

what is the one thing that, if i do it today,
makes today count toward the mission?

one thing. not a list. on days you do nothing else, you did that. that's what "wake up with a mission" actually means in practice: a mission is useless as a feeling and powerful as a daily decision.

the weekly receipt

every sunday, ten minutes. this is what stops the gap from feeling discouraging: you can see you're closing it.

WEEKLY RECEIPT                         week of __________

the one thing, each day:   M  T  W  T  F  S  S
                           _  _  _  _  _  _  _

something i can do now that i couldn't a month ago:
_____________________________________________________

proof i'm moving (a number, a message, a first):
_____________________________________________________

next week's one change: ______________________________

stack enough receipts and day one turns into day one hundred. we're all human, and you can be amazing. now go work at it. that's Off-Script Rich.

good to know

  • their story is a highlight reel. the version of anyone's start that gets told years later is cleaner than it was. use it as proof the path exists, not as a timeline you're failing.
  • one thing beats ten. the morning question only works if the answer is small enough to actually do. if it takes more than two hours, it's a project, so cut it down.
  • skip a day, not two. missing once is normal. the receipt makes a second missed day visible, which is usually enough to stop a third.
