the raise plan

hey, it's KP. you commented SCRIPT, so here it is. the skit showed two friends with the same raise. this is the plan friend B was running, written out so you can use it the next time your pay goes up, with a calculator for what one raise turns into.

the idea

lifestyle creep isn't spending money. it's spending all of every raise automatically, so your costs rise exactly as fast as your pay. you never feel richer, because you never are. the fix isn't refusing the upgrade. it's deciding the split before the money arrives.

the plan

EVERY RAISE, BEFORE THE FIRST NEW PAYCHECK:

1. work out the monthly difference after tax:  $______
2. split it:
   ___% (i use 50%)  -> building: automatic transfer on payday
   ___% (i use 50%)  -> living better, guilt-free
3. building money goes in this order:
   a. emergency fund, until it hits its target
   b. any employer 401k match you're not getting yet
   c. a roth ira (comment ROTH for the 2026 limits)
   d. a side project or skill that adds another income
4. change the automatic transfer the same day.

the order matters. the match is free money, so it comes early. the side project is how friend B's side income passed his rent.

good to know

  • the 50/50 is mine, not a rule. if you're behind on the emergency fund, send more to building. if you've been tight for years, send more to living. the point is deciding.
  • returns aren't guaranteed. investments go down as well as up. the calculator is for testing ideas, not planning on a number.
  • check the tax. a $10,000 raise isn't $10,000 a year in your account. use the change on your pay stub.

proof

  • Investor.gov (SEC): compound interest calculator · check the math yourself
  • IRS: IRA contribution limits
