what a flipped congress can do to your money

hey, it's KP. you commented CONGRESS, so here it is. here's the one-page breakdown of what the new congress can and can't do, and the one thing i'd do with my own 401k this week. no party takes, just the mechanics.

what happened

in the 3 November 2026 midterms, major networks projected that democrats will control both the house and the senate. the new congress, the 120th, is sworn in on 3 January 2027. until then the current congress is still in charge for the lame-duck session.

the president is still from the other party, so this is a split government for the next two years.

what the new majority gets

  • the agenda. the majority picks which bills come up for a vote and who chairs every committee.
  • investigations. committee chairs can hold hearings and issue subpoenas.
  • the money. spending bills have to pass both chambers, so every budget now needs their votes.
  • confirmations. the senate confirms cabinet picks and federal judges, so the president's nominees need the new majority.

what doesn't change

the president still has the veto. overriding it takes two-thirds of both chambers. reporting on the results says the new majority isn't close to veto-proof. so the most likely outcome is two years where big new laws are hard to pass from either side. washington calls it gridlock.

for your money, that mostly means fewer surprises. tax rules already in law stay in law unless both sides agree to change them.

what markets have done after midterms

since 1950 there have been 19 midterms. 12 months later the s&p 500 was higher all 19 times, about +15% on average. that's a small sample and not a promise, and plenty of those years had a 10%+ drop along the way. the full table is on the HISTORY guide.

that's Off-Script Rich: read the history before the headline.

what i'd do with a 401k this week

  1. log in and check your contribution rate. that's the lever you control. the election isn't.
  2. don't move your allocation because of a headline. if you'd be fine holding it for five years, you're fine holding it this week.
  3. if a policy change would genuinely hit your job or industry, that's a reason to build your emergency fund, not a reason to sell your index fund.

the prompt

Congress just changed hands after the 2026 midterms, with the president from the other party.
Explain in plain words, for a regular person with a 401k and a savings account:
1. What a new congressional majority can and can't change without the president's signature.
2. Which kinds of laws are most likely to stall in a split government.
3. What I should watch in the news over the next 2 years that could actually affect my taxes or retirement account.
Stay non-partisan. Don't predict the stock market.

good to know

  • projections, then certification. a few races can take days or weeks to finish counting. the control of each chamber was projected by the networks on election night.
  • non-partisan on purpose. this guide is about mechanics. it doesn't say who should have won.
  • history is context, not a forecast. 19 for 19 is a streak, not a law of nature.
