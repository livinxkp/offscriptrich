how to actually use all three

hey, it's KP. you commented TRAVEL, so here it is. all three features, the prompts that make each one work, the exact partner list behind them, and the limits nobody puts in the announcement post.

who this is for

you, if you book two or more trips a year and you've been doing it by opening eight tabs and hoping. skip this if you're in the EEA — two of the three are explicitly not available there yet, and i'd rather tell you now than three sections in.

why this matters

google announced these on 27 August 2026, and the reason they matter isn't that they're new. it's that all three sit inside one conversation instead of three separate products. you used to track a fare in Google Flights, check award space on the airline's own site, and price hotels somewhere else entirely.

here's the thing to actually take away: the flight side tracks, the hotel side books. that asymmetry is the whole map of what's live today, and most of the coverage blurs it.

one · flight price tracking, without leaving the chat

set an alert mid-conversation and google emails you when the fare moves.

this is Google Flights' existing price tracking, wired into AI Mode. the underlying search covers more than 300 partner airlines and travel sites across 180-plus countries and territories, and once you're tracking a route you get an email when the price changes meaningfully. you can track specific dates, or leave the dates open and get told when the route's minimum drops.

to get there: go to google.com/ai, or search on google.com and tap AI Mode, or open AI Mode in the Google app. you don't strictly need to be signed in to use AI Mode — but for a tracked fare to reach your inbox, obviously you do.

I want to fly from [your airport] to [destination], leaving
some time in [month] and coming back 7 to 10 days later.
I'm flexible on exact dates. Economy, 1 adult, carry-on only,
and I'd rather not connect more than once.

Show me the current options with the fare on each, then
track these so I get an email if the price moves.

the flexible-dates version is where this earns its place. a fixed-date alert tells you about one flight. an open-dates alert tells you when the route gets interesting, which is how you end up going somewhere in a week you hadn't considered.

one habit that makes the whole thing worth setting up: run three alerts, not one. your obvious destination, a second city you'd genuinely go to instead, and the same trip out of your second-nearest airport. you're not trying to catch one fare. you're building a small feed of moving prices so that when one of them drops you already know whether it's a good number, because you've been watching the normal one for six weeks.

two · price it in points instead of money

ask in miles and it answers in miles — for the programmes that have signed up.

AI Mode can now show what a matching flight or hotel costs in loyalty currency, using data google says comes directly from its partners. this is the feature with the biggest gap between what it sounds like and what it currently does, so here is the actual list.

live at launch: Alaska Airlines, American Airlines, Choice Hotels, Hilton, Wyndham. google says these follow in the weeks after: Accor, Flying Blue, Hyatt, LATAM, Lufthansa Group.

that's it. it is not a universal award search. if your points sit with a programme that isn't on that list, this feature has nothing to tell you about them yet, and no amount of prompting changes that. check the list first, then decide whether to bother.

How many miles would I need to book a one-way from [origin]
to [destination] in [month] using [name the specific programme,
e.g. Alaska Mileage Plan]?

Show me the cash price next to the miles price for the same
flights, and tell me what each point is worth in cents if I
book it that way.

ask for the cash figure alongside the miles every single time. a miles number on its own is not information — 60,000 points is either excellent or terrible depending entirely on what the cash ticket costs that day, and you can only see which by putting the two side by side.

the arithmetic is one line: cash price in cents, divided by the number of points. that gives you cents per point, and once you've run it a dozen times on your own programme you develop a number you trust. below it you pay cash and keep the points. above it you burn them. you don't need anyone's valuation chart for that — you need your own, built from the routes you actually fly.

three · book the hotel without leaving the conversation

this is the one that actually completes a transaction, and it's the most geographically limited.

you describe the trip, it compares hotels with reviews and pricing in the conversation, and eligible reservations finish through a "Continue on Google" flow using Google Pay. it rolled out in the United States, in English, and google's own footnotes say some of this isn't available in EEA territories.

the booking and hotel partners named at launch: Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com, Priceline, Trip.com, and on the hotel side Choice Hotels, Hilton, IHG, Marriott, Wyndham. anything outside that set — an independent property, a smaller regional chain — you'll still be finishing on someone else's site.

Find me a hotel in [city], [check-in date] to [check-out date],
2 adults, one room.

Constraints:
- under [amount] per night all-in, including taxes and fees
- in or walking distance of [neighbourhood]
- rating 8.5 or above with at least 500 reviews
- free cancellation up to 48 hours before

Show me the five best matches side by side with the total for
the whole stay, not the nightly rate. Flag anything with a
resort fee or a mandatory city tax not in the headline price.

two things make that prompt work. total for the stay, not the nightly rate — nightly pricing is how a place that looks 20 a night better ends up 90 worse once taxes land. and the free-cancellation line, because a refundable booking made now is a floor you can improve on later, not a decision you're stuck with.

none of this is a hack. it's just doing the comparison properly once, in one place, instead of eight tabs and a feeling. getting the same trip for less and spending the difference on being there longer — that's Off-Script Rich.

good to know

  • there's a fourth one nobody mentions. Flight Deals is a separate AI tool inside Google Flights where you describe a trip in words instead of filling a form. it surfaces fares at least 20% below the typical price for that route over the previous 12 months. it's explicitly experimental, and it won't handle multi-city itineraries, groups over four, infants, or layover preferences.
  • the partner lists are the feature. both the points pricing and the hotel booking only work for companies that have integrated. it isn't a search of the whole market wearing an AI hat, and treating it as one is how you conclude "it's wrong" when it's actually just incomplete.
  • a tracked fare is not a held fare. the alert tells you the price moved. it doesn't reserve anything, and google's own wording on the deals side is that prices change often and what you see may change when you go to book. speed still matters.
  • AI Mode itself is in over 200 countries and dozens of languages — the travel features are not. the fact that AI Mode answers you in your country tells you nothing about whether hotel booking works there. it launched US and English.
