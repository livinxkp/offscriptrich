six prompts for linkedin headshots

hey, it's KP. you commented SHOTS, so here it is. the six prompts from the reel, in full, with the colour palettes that came with them. copy one, paste it into whatever image tool you already use, swap the bracket for yourself. about ten minutes for all six.

why this matters

your profile picture is the one image of you that anyone looking you up will definitely see, and it is the only one on the page you completely control. everything else — your title, your company, who you know — is either true or it isn't. the photo is a choice you make.

until recently the choice was pay a photographer, or crop yourself out of a wedding photo and hope. there is a third option now, and the gap between an ai headshot that passes and one that looks like a mannequin is almost entirely the prompt. vague in, plastic out. the prompts that work are specific about the boring things: where the light is coming from, what the fabric is doing, how far away the camera is, what colours are allowed in the frame.

that is what these six are. each one is long on purpose.

the setup

01

pick your tool

any image model that lets you attach a reference photo. use whichever you already have open. these prompts are written in plain description, so nothing here depends on one tool's syntax.

02

feed it your face

three to five clear photos of you. front-on, daylight, no sunglasses, no heavy filter, slightly different angles. bad references are the single biggest reason outputs come back looking like someone else.

03

swap the bracket

every prompt opens with [a South Asian man in his late twenties]. replace what's inside the brackets with you, brackets and all. leave the rest alone — the detail is what's doing the work.

good to know

  • the bracket isn't the only thing describing you — several of these also say dark hair, light stubble, medium-brown skin. if that isn't you, edit those words too, or the model will quietly drift back toward the description instead of your reference photos.
  • check the hands and the glasses — fingers and lens reflections are still where image models fall over. generate three or four per prompt and bin the broken ones. it costs you nothing but a minute.
  • the hex codes are a steer, not a law — they nudge the palette toward the look from the reel. some tools respect them closely, some barely notice. if your output ignores them entirely, that's the tool, not the prompt.
  • the one most people get wrong — they let it make them better looking instead of better lit. if someone who has met you wouldn't recognise you from the photo, it has failed at the only job it had. regenerate.
