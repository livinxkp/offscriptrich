the five-hobby system

hey, it's KP. you commented HOBBY, so here it is. the reel gave you the five pillars. this is the worksheet: how to pick one hobby per pillar that you'll actually keep, how to fit all five into a normal week, and why the people one matters more than it sounds.

who this is for

you, if you have a long list of things you'd like to try and a short list of things you actually do. skip it if you're already doing something from all five. you've built the system, you just didn't call it one.

the five pillars

  • body. keeps you healthy. lifting, boxing, pilates, yoga, running, climbing, dancing. the bar from public health guidance is at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week plus two days of muscle strengthening, so pick something that gets you there.
  • create. something made with your hands or your head: an instrument, cooking, drawing, writing, building.
  • peace. no phone, no output: hiking, walking, meditating, fishing, gardening. the rule is that it produces nothing.
  • people. connects you to others: a team sport, a run club, volunteering, a weekly dinner, travelling with friends.
  • growth. grows you as a person: reading, a language, journaling, a course. the one most people skip.

how to pick

for each pillar, a hobby has to pass three tests or it won't last:

1. can i start it this week with what i already have, or under $50?
2. can i do it in under an hour, near where i live?
3. would i still do it if nobody ever saw it?

three yeses = keep it. any no = pick something else.

fitting five into a week

you don't need five separate time slots. stack them:

MON  body (45 min)
TUE  growth (20 min, before bed)
WED  body + people (run club, climbing gym, team sport)
THU  create (45 min)
FRI  growth (20 min)
SAT  peace (long walk, no phone) + body
SUN  people (cook for someone = create + people)

the overlap is the trick. cooking dinner for friends covers two pillars. a hike with someone covers three.

why the people one matters

it's the easiest to skip because it needs other people to show up. it's also the one with the strongest evidence behind it. a meta-analysis of 148 studies covering over 300,000 people found that stronger social relationships were linked to a 50% higher likelihood of survival over the study periods, an effect comparable to quitting smoking. the US Surgeon General put out a whole advisory on loneliness in 2023.

so you don't need twenty hobbies. you need one from each pillar. which one are you missing? that's Off-Script Rich.

good to know

  • one at a time. if all five are new, add one a week. starting five at once is how you end up with none.
  • seasons change it. it's fine for the body hobby to be running in summer and climbing in winter. the pillar stays, the activity can rotate.
  • the guidance numbers are for adults in general. if you have a health condition, check with a doctor before starting something intense.

proof

  • CDC: physical activity guidelines for adults · 150 minutes of moderate activity and two days of muscle strengthening a week
  • Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton (2010), Social relationships and mortality risk: a meta-analytic review, PLOS Medicine · 148 studies, 308,849 participants, 50% higher likelihood of survival
  • US Surgeon General (2023): Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation · the advisory
