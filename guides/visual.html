the images 2.5 prompt library

hey, it's KP. you commented VISUAL, so here it is. the reel showed you what people are making. this is where the prompts actually live, how to lift one without getting a worse copy of someone else's image, and six starter prompts i wrote for the shots brands and creators actually need.

who this is for

you, if you run a brand, make content, or sell anything that needs pictures, and you've been paying for shoots or settling for stock. skip this if you need a real photo of a real product you have to show accurately. generated images are for mood, concept and content, not for the listing photo a customer relies on.

what actually changed in 2.5

OpenAI shipped ChatGPT Images 2.5 on 8 September 2026, to every ChatGPT tier on web, desktop and mobile. the parts that matter for content:

  • precision editing. it changes only what you ask for and leaves the rest of the image alone. this is the one that makes it usable for brand work.
  • it holds up over long chats. earlier edits stay intact across many turns instead of drifting.
  • faster. OpenAI says generation latency is down by up to 50% against Images 2.0.
  • @Sketch and templates. type @Sketch to draw a rough layout as a guide, or start from formats like Poster and Merch.
  • you can share the prompt behind an image. which is exactly why public prompt libraries exploded the same week.

where the prompts live

two libraries, both free to browse.

YouMind's GPT Image 2.5 library is the one from the reel. when i checked it listed 1,091 prompts, and the count climbs daily. filter by use case (product marketing, e-commerce main image, poster, YouTube thumbnail, social post), by style (photography, cinematic film still, 3D render, minimalism) or by subject. every prompt has the image it made next to it, so you're picking by result, not by description.

the same team keeps a bigger archive on GitHub, awesome-gpt-image-2, built for the previous model. it lists over 17,000 prompts with sample images and the original author credited. most of them still work in 2.5, and 2.5 usually does them better.

how to steal a prompt properly

most people paste the whole thing, swap nothing, and get a slightly worse version of someone else's picture. do this instead.

  1. pick by the light, not the subject. find an image whose lighting and camera feel is what you want, even if it's a sneaker and you sell candles.
  2. keep the camera and light language, swap the subject. phrases like "85mm, f/1.8, soft window light from the left, shallow depth of field" are what carry the look. the subject line is the only part that's really theirs.
  3. add your brand constraints last. your colours, your product's real shape and material, your aspect ratio (4:5 for feed, 9:16 for stories and reels).
  4. edit, don't regenerate. with 2.5, ask for one change at a time ("make the background warmer, change nothing else"). regenerating from scratch throws away everything that was already right.

six starter prompts

fill in the brackets. each one is written so the look survives when you change the subject.

STUDIO SHOOT
A studio portrait of [person or product], seamless [colour] paper
backdrop, one large softbox key light at 45 degrees camera left,
subtle fill from the right, 85mm lens, f/2.8, crisp focus on
[the eyes / the label], natural skin texture, no retouched plastic
look, editorial magazine quality, 4:5 vertical.
PRODUCT CAMPAIGN
A hero campaign image of [product, with its real colour and
material], placed on [surface: travertine block / brushed steel /
linen], hard afternoon sunlight casting a long sharp shadow, a few
[props that fit the brand] at the edge of frame, generous empty
space in the top third for headline text, high-end commercial
photography, 4:5 vertical.
EDITORIAL
An editorial fashion photograph of [subject] in [location], shot
on medium format film, muted colour grade with slightly lifted
blacks, candid in-between moment rather than a posed smile,
[wardrobe details], natural overcast light, grain visible, styled
like a print magazine spread, 3:2 horizontal.
CAMERA ROLL
A casual iPhone photo of [scene], taken quickly by a friend, slightly
off-centre framing, mild motion blur on the hands, phone flash in
low light OR harsh midday sun, imperfect white balance, ordinary
background details left in, no cinematic grading, looks like an
unedited camera roll photo, 3:4 vertical.
POSTER
A minimalist poster for [event or product]. Large bold headline
reading exactly "[HEADLINE]" in a condensed sans serif, small
supporting line reading exactly "[DATE OR DETAIL]". [one strong
image idea] as the only illustration. Two-colour palette:
[colour] and [colour] on off-white. Lots of negative space.
Print-ready, 2:3 vertical.
THUMBNAIL
A YouTube thumbnail, 16:9. [Person] on the right third with a
[specific expression], looking at [the object] on the left. Text
reading exactly "[3 TO 5 WORDS]" in heavy white sans serif with a
thin black outline, top left. High contrast, saturated but not
neon, readable at phone size, no extra small text.

for any text in the image, put the exact words in quotes and say "reading exactly". it cuts misspellings a lot, and you still check every letter before posting.

the edit loop that makes it brand-ready

once you have an image you like, stay in the same chat and run these one at a time.

Keep everything about this image the same. Only change [one thing].
Make this match our brand: [hex colours], [mood in three words].
Do not change the composition, the subject or the lighting direction.
Give me the same image as a 9:16 vertical version. Extend the
background naturally. Keep the subject the same size and position
relative to the frame centre.

three edits beats thirty regenerations. that's Off-Script Rich: steal the method, not the picture.

good to know

  • check every letter. text rendering is much better than it was and it still slips. read the headline character by character before it goes anywhere public.
  • camera-roll content is a disclosure question. an image built to look like a real unedited photo is fine for mood and storytelling. using it as proof that something happened, or as a customer photo, is where it stops being content and starts being misleading.
  • real people need real permission. don't generate recognisable real people, or put someone's face into a scene, without their consent. OpenAI's usage policies apply to everything you make in ChatGPT.
  • the counts move. the 1,091 and 17,000+ numbers are what the libraries showed on 12 October 2026. both grow daily, so treat them as a floor.
