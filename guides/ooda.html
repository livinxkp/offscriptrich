life OODA

the ooda loop, for normal decisions

hey, it's KP. you commented OODA, so here it is. the reel gave you the four words. this is the worksheet: what each step actually asks, three worked examples (a text, a pitch, a purchase), and a 60-second version for when your brain is spiralling.

who this is for

you, if you've ever spent an hour on a two-line message, or put off a decision until it made itself. skip it for decisions that genuinely need time and other people: a move, a job, a relationship. this is for the small calls you're overthinking.

where it comes from

the OODA loop is from John Boyd, a US Air Force fighter pilot and strategist. his point was that in a fight, the side that cycles through observe, orient, decide, act faster, and updates as the situation changes, wins. it later spread into business and sport.

the part most people skip is orient. Boyd treated it as the most important step: it's where your experience, your assumptions and the new facts meet. overthinking is usually being stuck there, replaying stories instead of reading what's actually in front of you.

the four steps

OBSERVE   what are the actual facts?
          (not the story, not what they "probably meant")

ORIENT    what matters most here?
          (what's at stake, what's the deadline, what do i want)

DECIDE    what's the smallest next move?
          (not the whole plan. the next step.)

ACT       do it now, before your brain invents ten more outcomes.
          then loop: what happened? observe again.

three worked examples

the text you're overthinking

OBSERVE  they replied "sounds good" 4 hours ago. that's all.
ORIENT   i want to confirm friday. nothing is actually wrong.
DECIDE   send one line confirming the time.
ACT      "great, friday 7 at the usual place?" sent.

the pitch you've been avoiding

OBSERVE  due thursday. it's monday. i have the brief.
ORIENT   less stress tomorrow if it exists in any form today.
DECIDE   write the first three lines.
ACT      open the doc. three lines. now.

the purchase you keep researching

OBSERVE  two options, $40 apart, both well reviewed.
ORIENT   i'll use it daily. returns are free for 30 days.
DECIDE   buy the one with the feature i'd actually use.
ACT      order it. stop reading reviews.

the 60-second version

when your head is spinning, say it out loud or type it:

the facts are ______.
what matters is ______.
the smallest next move is ______.
i'm doing it now.

overthinking drains you because your brain is hunting for the perfect move before it lets you make any move. you don't need perfect. you need the next step, and then another loop. that's Off-Script Rich.

good to know

  • fast isn't reckless. the loop is fast because each move is small and reversible. if the next move can't be undone, slow down and loop more carefully.
  • orient is where bias lives. if every loop lands on the same anxious story, write the facts and the story in two separate columns. it's usually obvious which is which.
  • the act step creates new information. that's the point. you learn more from the reply to a sent message than from rereading an unsent one.

proof

  • Air University Press: John R. Boyd, A Discourse on Winning and Losing · Boyd's briefings, edited and published by the US Air Force's Air University
