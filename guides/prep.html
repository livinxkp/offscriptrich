the 2027 prep checklist

hey, it's KP. you commented PREP, so here it is. nobody knows what 2027 does. this is the checklist i'm running so i don't have to know: the emergency fund math for la rent with a calculator, plus the other two moves from the reel.

how a slowdown spreads

companies cut jobs. people who lose jobs miss payments. people still working get nervous and spend less, which hits more companies. you can't stop that loop. you can make sure it doesn't reach your rent.

move one: the emergency fund

a common rule of thumb is three to six months of essential costs. the CFPB's own guide says the right number depends on your situation. i use six because my income isn't one paycheck. for reference, HUD's 2026 fair market rent for a one-bedroom in the Los Angeles area is $2,328 a month, so six months of rent alone is nearly $14,000 before anything else.

your number

your emergency fund target$0

keep it in a high-yield savings account at an FDIC-insured bank, separate from your checking so you don't spend it. comment SAFE on that reel for how i hold mine.

move two: no new monthly payments

  • no new car loan or lease.
  • no buy now pay later.
  • no new subscriptions without cancelling one.

every fixed payment is something a pay cut can't negotiate with. fewer of them means a rough year hurts less.

move three: income that doesn't depend on one employer

this is the one most people skip. it doesn't have to be big. a skill you can sell on the side, a small client, a product. the point is that if one stream stops, the others keep going. if you want ideas that don't need a product, comment MIDDLE on that reel.

good to know

  • this isn't a prediction. it's what i'd do in any year. if 2027 is great, you lose nothing by being ready.
  • fair market rent isn't your rent. HUD's number is a benchmark. put your actual rent in the calculator.
  • don't stop retirement contributions to build this if your employer matches. take the match, build the fund with what's left.
