you're not behind on ai. here's the map

hey, it's KP. you commented AI, so here it is. four things, in order: one course for the concepts, one course for working with it, and two free tools to open every day. then a seven-day plan at twenty minutes a day, with the prompts to paste.

who this is for

you, if it feels like everyone else got a briefing you missed and you don't know where to start. skip this if you already use AI daily in your work. you'll find the first half obvious, and the thirty-day plan is the better next step.

why this matters

feeling behind usually isn't a skill gap. it's that nobody gave you an order. the loudest corner of the internet makes AI look like a hundred tools and a new release every day, so you open one tab, ask one vague question, get a vague answer, and close it.

the fix is a short list. the concepts course below has been taken by over two million people from more than 170 countries, and it needs no programming. the working-with-AI course is four hours, and both are free.

the map

01 · the concepts: Elements of AI. made by MinnaLearn and the University of Helsinki. the first part, Introduction to AI, covers what AI is, what it can and can't do, and how it affects daily life, with no programming. it's self-paced and free. do this one first so the words stop sounding like a foreign language.

02 · working with it: AI Fluency, from Anthropic. four hours, 14 lessons and a quiz, free. it teaches one framework, the 4Ds: delegation (what to hand off), description (how to ask), discernment (how to judge the answer) and diligence (how to use it responsibly). you can start without signing in, and an account saves your progress and lets you earn a completion badge.

03 · tool one: Claude. there's a free plan at $0. it includes chat on web, desktop and mobile, web search, file creation, memory across conversations and the ability to connect apps. this is where you think, draft and ask.

04 · tool two: Gemini. also free, and it needs only a Google Account. the free tier lists Deep Research and Gemini Notebook for research and writing help. this is your second opinion, and the place for anything that touches your Google stuff.

two tools, not ten. the point of the map is that it ends.

the seven-day plan

twenty minutes a day. stop when the timer stops. the people who binge it on a sunday are gone by thursday.

day 1  start Elements of AI. one chapter. no notes.
day 2  next chapter. open Claude and ask it to explain
       one idea you just read (prompt 1 below).
day 3  start AI Fluency. watch only the first lessons.
day 4  tell Claude who you are and what you do (prompt 2).
day 5  hand it one real task from your week (prompt 3).
day 6  open Gemini. ask it the same question you asked
       Claude and compare the two answers.
day 7  review: what did you learn, what do you do next
       week (prompt 4).

prompt 1 · the translator

for any word or idea in the courses that didn't land.

I'm new to AI and I just read about [TERM OR IDEA].
Explain it in plain English in under 120 words, like I'm
smart but have never worked in tech. Give me one everyday
example, then ask me one question to check I understood it.
Don't use jargon without defining it.

prompt 2 · the introduction

the single biggest upgrade for a beginner is telling it who you are. paste this once and answer its questions.

I want you to understand my situation before we do
anything. Here is who I am: [WHAT YOU DO AND WHO PAYS YOU].
Here's what I spend most of my week on: [THREE THINGS].
Ask me up to five questions, one at a time, about my work
and goals. When you're done, summarise what you know about
me in five lines so I can check it's right.

prompt 3 · the hand-off

this is the delegation step from the AI Fluency course, run on your own week.

Here is a task I do regularly: [DESCRIBE IT].
Tell me which parts you can do well, which parts you'll
likely get wrong, and which parts only I should do.
Then do the first part. I'll tell you what to fix.

prompt 4 · the weekly check

This week I [WHAT YOU TRIED]. Ask me three questions about
what worked and what didn't. Then give me one small thing to
try next week, based on my answers. One thing, not a list.

good to know

  • free plans have limits, and they change. what each free plan includes is whatever its pricing page says today. check it before you build a habit around a feature.
  • check what it tells you. AI can state wrong things confidently. judging the answer is the third D in the AI Fluency course for a reason. if it matters, verify it somewhere else.
  • don't paste what you wouldn't email to a stranger. bank details, passwords, other people's private information. use the tools on your own work first.
  • finishing the courses isn't the goal. using the tools on real tasks is. the courses are what make that feel less random.
