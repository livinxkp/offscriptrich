two middleman models you can start this month

hey, it's KP. you commented MIDDLE, so here it is. the reel was the idea. this is how to actually start the two middleman models from it this month, without building anything, plus the rule you have to follow so it stays legal.

the idea

in 2015 the strategist Tom Goodwin wrote that Uber, the world's largest taxi company, owns no vehicles, and Airbnb, the world's largest accommodation provider, owns no real estate. both make money by standing between someone who has something and someone who needs it. you can do a small version of that without building an app.

model one: affiliate

how it pays: you recommend a product you actually use, with your own link. when someone buys through it, the company pays you a commission.

  1. list the 5 tools or products you already use and would recommend to a friend.
  2. search "[product] affiliate program" for each one. most software and many brands have one.
  3. make one honest piece of content per product: how you use it, what it's bad at.
  4. put the link where people can find it, with the disclosure (below).

the rule: the FTC says you have to disclose any financial relationship with a brand, including affiliate commissions and free products. put it with the recommendation, in plain words like "i earn a commission if you buy through my link". in a video, say it and show it. a platform's disclosure tool alone isn't enough.

model two: appointment setting

how it pays: businesses that run ads or sell high-ticket services get more leads than they can call. an appointment setter contacts those leads and books calls for the closer. pay is usually a base, per booked call, or a share of closed deals. agree it in writing before you start.

  1. pick an industry you understand (fitness coaches, med spas, agencies, real estate).
  2. find businesses already running ads. the Meta Ad Library shows who is running ads right now.
  3. offer a short paid trial: "i'll follow up on your unanswered leads for two weeks".
  4. track every call you book. that number is your pitch for the next client.

the middle you already have

if people already watch you, your attention is the middle. that's what brand deals and affiliate links pay for. that's Off-Script Rich: the money sits in between.

the prompts

1) I already use these products: [list]. For each, tell me if it has an affiliate program, where to sign up, and how commission typically works. If you're not sure a program exists, say so.

2) I want to offer appointment setting to [industry] businesses in [city]. Write me a 4-sentence DM offering a 2-week paid trial following up on their unanswered leads. Plain, no hype.

3) Write a one-line affiliate disclosure for an Instagram reel and a spoken version I can say in the first 10 seconds.

good to know

  • neither is instant. affiliate takes an audience or search traffic. appointment setting takes a client willing to trust you. both are real, neither is passive at the start.
  • watch for pay-to-join offers. a legit setter role pays you. if someone wants you to buy a course before you can work, that's a different business.
  • only recommend what you use. it's better business, and the FTC is clear that endorsements have to reflect your honest opinion.

proof
