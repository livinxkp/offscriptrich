ten hobbies, each one in under 20 minutes

hey, it's KP. you commented HOBBIES, so here it is. the reel got through six. here's the full list of ten, and for each one exactly what to practise in your first 20 minutes so you finish with something you can actually show someone, plus where to learn it free.

who this is for

you, if your free time mostly turns into scrolling and you'd like your hands and brain doing something else. skip it if you want a hobby to become a business. these are for being a more interesting person, not a more productive one.

the ten

1. handwriting. 20 minutes: slow down to half speed and write one sentence ten times, keeping letters the same height and slant. lined paper, any pen. the trick is speed, not talent.

2. card shuffling. 20 minutes: the riffle shuffle and the bridge, the move where the cards cascade back together. split the deck evenly, bend from the corners, release with your thumbs. it'll look rough for a day, then suddenly it won't.

3. tying knots. 20 minutes: five knots that cover most of life. the bowline (a loop that won't slip), the clove hitch (tie to a post), the taut-line hitch (adjustable tension), the sheet bend (join two ropes), the trucker's hitch (strap anything down). Animated Knots shows each one step by step, free.

4. pen spinning. 20 minutes: the thumbaround, where the pen spins once around your thumb and lands back in place. practise over a bed or a couch. it's the base move for almost everything else.

5. studying human behaviour. 20 minutes: pick one idea and look for it today. good starters: people mirror the posture of someone they like, and people tell you what they care about by what they bring up twice. put a psychology audiobook on while you walk and test one idea per day.

6. calling someone. 20 minutes: message someone you haven't talked to in a year. not "we should catch up". something specific: "saw this and thought of you". then actually call. people are consistently more pleased to hear from you than you expect them to be, and that's been measured.

7. juggling. 20 minutes: one ball, hand to hand, eye height, for five minutes. then two. then the three-ball cascade. three rolled socks work fine.

8. a memory palace. 20 minutes: picture your home. walk through it in order and "place" one item from a list in each spot, as a weird vivid image. then walk back through and read them off. try it on a ten-item list.

9. sketching. 20 minutes: draw one object on your desk five times, faster each time. last one in 60 seconds. you're training your eye to see shapes, not making art.

10. touch typing. 20 minutes: home row only. fingers on asdf and jkl;, don't look down. free typing sites will drill you. slow and right beats fast and wrong for the first week.

pick three, not ten

the mistake is starting all ten. pick three, one for each:

HANDS   (shuffling, knots, pen spinning, juggling, sketching)  ______
MIND    (memory palace, typing, human behaviour)               ______
PEOPLE  (calling someone, every week)                          ______

20 minutes a day, rotate. in a month you'll have three things
you can actually do, instead of ten you tried once.

the phone stays in another room while you practise. the whole point is twenty minutes it doesn't get. that's Off-Script Rich.

good to know

  • the first 20 minutes are the worst ones. every one of these looks clumsy at first. judge it after a week, not after a session.
  • knots are only useful if they're right. if you're ever trusting a knot with weight, or with a person, learn it from a proper source and check it every time. a nearly-right knot is the dangerous kind.
  • calling is the one with the biggest payoff. it's also the one people skip. do it first.
