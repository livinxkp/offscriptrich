the faceless page stack, honestly

hey, it's KP. you commented FACELESS, so here it is. the link to the tool from the reel, the version you can assemble yourself for about $11 a month, and the two rules that decide whether any of it ever pays you.

who this is for

this is for you if you want a page running daily while you're at work, and you're fine being a producer instead of a performer. skip it if your goal is brand deals — sponsors buy a face and a voice they trust, and a faceless page sells ad revenue and products instead.

why this matters

the reel showed you the setup. what it couldn't fit is the part that decides everything: every platform now pays on a threshold, and two of them have explicit rules about mass-produced content. you can automate 60 uploads a month and still earn nothing, because the money doesn't come from posting. it comes from clearing a specific number and staying inside a specific policy.

so this page is the stack, the real prices, and then the two numbers and one policy line you should build the whole thing around. read the last two sections even if you skip the rest.

the tool from the reel

it's facelessreels.com. its own listing describes it as a platform that automates the creation and the posting of faceless videos across TikTok, Instagram and YouTube, with niche presets like history, true crime and anime. the "series" flow in the reel is the whole point of it — you configure a niche, voice, style and posting time once, and it keeps writing, generating, voicing and publishing on that schedule without you opening it again.

one honest note before you pay for anything: at the time i checked, it had no verified reviews on G2 at all. that isn't a red flag by itself — it's a new category and most of these tools are young — but it does mean you're the review. start on the smallest plan, run one series for two weeks, and only scale the spend after you've seen what it actually publishes.

the same stack, assembled yourself

build it from parts and you own the output, the voice and the schedule separately — so no single tool shutting down takes your page with it. three pieces do the whole job:

  • script + visuals: invideo AI generates the script, pulls stock footage from its library (iStock, Storyblocks and Shutterstock feeds), adds voiceover, music and sound effects from one prompt. free to start.
  • voice: ElevenLabs. the free tier is 10,000 credits a month and does not include commercial rights. the Starter plan at $6/month is the first tier that carries a commercial licence, at 30,000 credits. the next tier up, Creator, is $22/month at regular price for 121,000 credits, with the first month half off.
  • posting: Buffer publishes to Instagram, TikTok and YouTube Shorts. free plan covers 3 channels with 10 scheduled posts per channel that refill as they publish; Essentials is $5/month per channel billed annually.

so the working monthly floor is about $11 — ElevenLabs Starter plus one Buffer channel — with invideo's free tier doing the assembly. that's the same job the all-in-one does, with the failure risk spread across three companies instead of one.

the setup, exactly

run this once. it takes about 40 minutes and it's the only part you can't automate.

FACELESS PAGE — SETUP CHECKLIST

[ ] accounts: convert Instagram to a Professional (Business or
    Creator) account — personal accounts CANNOT auto-publish reels
[ ] pick ONE niche and one format. not three. one.
[ ] write the series brief (prompt below) and save it
[ ] voice: pick one and never change it — the voice IS the brand
[ ] video specs for auto-publish to Instagram:
      length 3 sec – 15 min | ratio between 4:5 and 9:16
      max width 1920px | file under 300 MB
[ ] target length: 65-75 seconds (see the length rule below)
[ ] NO licensed music on auto-published reels — it forces a manual
    publish and breaks your schedule
[ ] no links in the reel caption — they don't work and can fail
    the publish. put it in bio or first comment.
[ ] schedule: 2 posts/day = 60/month. set the times and leave it.
[ ] set a weekly 20-min slot in your calendar to review output

the series brief that makes it not-generic

the default output of every one of these tools is the same slideshow with a different voice, and that is exactly what gets demonetised. the fix is a brief you paste in once, with a point of view in it. this is the one i'd use:

SERIES BRIEF — paste into the tool's custom prompt field

niche: [one narrow topic, e.g. "shipwrecks of the north atlantic"
        NOT "history"]
angle: every episode argues something. never just recounts.
format for each 70-second script:
  0-3s   a specific claim that sounds wrong but is true
  3-15s  one concrete detail: a date, a name, a number
  15-50s the story, in short sentences, present tense
  50-65s the twist or the thing everyone gets wrong
  65-70s one line of my own opinion, stated plainly

rules:
- never open with "did you know" or "here's why"
- one idea per video. no lists.
- name real people, places and years. vagueness kills retention.
- end on a statement, not a question
- write at a reading age of 12. short words.

voice of the host: [3 adjectives + one thing they'd never say]

that last field is the one people skip and it's the one that matters. a stated point of view is the difference between "AI content" and "a channel".

the length rule, and what actually pays

60 to 70 seconds isn't a superstition — it's TikTok's threshold, and it's the only one of the three you can hit quickly. here are the real numbers, as each platform publishes them:

  • TikTok Creator Rewards: you must be 18+, have at least 10,000 followers, at least 100,000 video views in the last 30 days, and an account in good standing in an eligible region. it rewards original content over a minute long. that's why 65–75 seconds — a 58-second video earns nothing from this program.
  • YouTube Partner Program: two routes. 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months, or 1,000 subscribers plus 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days. Shorts watch time does not count toward the 4,000 hours. ten million in ninety days is roughly 110,000 views a day, every day. plan accordingly.
  • Instagram: there's no open per-view payout to qualify for. treat Instagram as the distribution and audience layer, and let TikTok and YouTube carry the direct revenue.

so the honest ranking: TikTok is the realistic first payout, YouTube is the long build, Instagram is reach. if you optimise for one thing in month one, optimise for TikTok's 10k followers and 100k views. here's the run plan i'd put on the calendar:

30-DAY RUN PLAN — one series, three platforms

week 1  publish 2/day. change NOTHING. you are gathering a baseline.
        record: 3-sec retention, avg watch %, follows per 1k views
week 2  keep the topic, test the first 3 seconds only.
        5 openers x 2 days each. keep the format identical.
week 3  take the single best opener from week 2 and make it the
        default in the series brief. now test length: 65s vs 90s.
week 4  double down on the best-performing sub-topic.
        write 5 episodes on it manually, in your own words, and
        feed them back in as style examples.

the one metric that decides whether to continue:
  follows per 1,000 views. under 2 = the content works and the
  page doesn't. over 5 = keep going, you have a channel.

what to ignore for 30 days: likes, comments, one viral outlier.

the line you can't cross

YouTube changed the name of this policy from "repetitious" to "inauthentic" in July 2025 and improved detection at the same time. the current channel monetization policy says AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates that gives the impression of mass production is ineligible for monetization without the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective. YouTube has been clear it isn't banning AI — using AI to write scripts, generate a background visual or visualise a character you invented is explicitly fine. what it's targeting is channels uploading narrative videos with only superficial differences between them, and slideshows with the same narration over and over.

which means the automation is legal and the laziness is not. the survivable version of a faceless page has three things a template doesn't: a narrow subject, a stated opinion, and variation between episodes. that's what the series brief above is for. if your last ten videos could be swapped for each other without anyone noticing, you're building the thing the policy was written about.

automation buys you the reps. it doesn't buy you the point of view. bring one, and a page you never appear on can still sound like somebody. that's Off-Script Rich.

good to know

  • adding licensed music to a reel silently breaks auto-publishing — Buffer switches it to notification publishing and waits for you to finish it by hand in the app. same for effects, product tags and collabs. a schedule that quietly needs you every day isn't a schedule.
  • ElevenLabs' free tier has no commercial licence. if you monetise a page voiced on the free plan you're outside the terms. the fix is $6/month, so just pay it before you post, not after.
  • Buffer's free plan is 10 scheduled posts per channel, refilling as they publish — fine for testing, not for 60 a month across three channels without babysitting the queue.
  • the 10-million-Shorts-views-in-90-days route to YouTube monetization is far harder than it sounds and most faceless channels never get near it. if YouTube is your goal, the 4,000-watch-hours route via longer videos is usually the shorter path.
  • run one series for two weeks before you add a second. the failure mode here isn't posting too little, it's three half-dead pages nobody follows.
