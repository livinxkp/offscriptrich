cash or finance: the one-page cheat sheet

hey, it's KP. you commented PAGE, so here it is. here's the page from the reel, all eight purchases with the reason for each, the one rule underneath them, and a blank version to run your own list through.

the rule

if it shrinks, pay cash. if it can grow, borrow smart.

things that lose value the day you buy them shouldn't come with interest attached. things that can earn or grow can sometimes justify a loan, as long as what it earns beats what the loan costs.

the car exception, with the math

this is the one most people get backwards. compare two numbers:

  1. the loan's APR on the dealer or bank offer.
  2. the APY on your high-yield savings account, which you'll find on your bank's rate page. it's variable, so check it on the day.

if the loan rate is lower and you'd actually leave the cash sitting in savings, financing can come out ahead. if the loan rate is higher, or you'd spend the cash, pay cash. the deal only works for disciplined people. be honest about which one you are.

the prompt

I'm deciding whether to pay cash or finance [item] costing $[price].
Loan offer: [APR]% for [months] months. My savings account pays [APY]%.
1. Total interest I'd pay on the loan.
2. Interest I'd earn if I kept the cash in savings for the same period instead.
3. Which is larger, and by how much.
Show the math. Don't factor in investment returns.

good to know

  • promo 0% deals have teeth. some "0% interest" offers charge all the deferred interest if you miss the payoff date. read the terms.
  • a degree isn't automatic. some raise earnings a lot, some barely. look up typical pay for the specific job, not the degree in general.
  • a house has costs beyond the loan. taxes, insurance and repairs are part of the real price.

proof

  • CFPB: auto loans · compare APRs and loan terms before you sign
  • CFPB: how to understand special promotional financing offers · deferred interest vs 0% promotions
