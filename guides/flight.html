the bumped-flight script

hey, it's KP. you commented FLIGHT, so here it is. here's the script from the skit, the exact words to say at the gate, plus the actual DOT rules behind them so you know what you're owed before the agent tells you.

the rules, domestic flights from the US

you arriveyou're owed
less than 1 hour latenothing
1 to 2 hours late200% of your one-way fare, up to $1,075
more than 2 hours late400% of your one-way fare, up to $2,150

international flights leaving the US use 1 to 4 hours for the 200% tier and more than 4 hours for the 400% tier, with the same caps. a $300 one-way ticket, more than 2 hours late, domestic: $1,200.

you only qualify if

  • you were bumped against your will. if you volunteer, you're negotiating instead, and the rules above don't apply.
  • you had a confirmed reservation.
  • you checked in on time and were at the gate on time.
  • you're flying from a US airport.

a few situations aren't covered, like a swap to a smaller plane or very small aircraft. check the DOT page if that's your situation.

the voucher

the law treats cash or a check as the default. an airline can offer a travel voucher instead, but it has to tell you that you can decline the voucher and take the cash or check. airlines have to pay at the airport the same day, or within 24 hours if your new flight leaves before they can.

the script

AGENT: we've moved you to the next flight.
YOU:   did i volunteer to give up my seat?
AGENT: no.
YOU:   then this is involuntary denied boarding.
       when does the new flight arrive compared to my original arrival?
AGENT: [x] hours later.
YOU:   my one-way fare was $[fare]. under DOT rules that's [200 / 400]%
       of the fare, so $[amount]. i'd like that as a check, please,
       not a voucher. can i also get the written statement of my rights?

stay polite. the agent didn't oversell the flight. that's Off-Script Rich: know the rule before they tell you the rule.

good to know

  • volunteering can pay more. if they're asking for volunteers, you can name your price. once they pick you involuntarily, the table above is your floor.
  • the caps change. the DOT adjusts the dollar caps for inflation. check the current numbers on transportation.gov before you fly.
  • keep your receipt. you'll need your fare amount, and it's on your booking confirmation.

proof

  • U.S. Department of Transportation: bumping and oversales · 200% up to $1,075, 400% up to $2,150, eligibility, same-day payment
  • 14 CFR 250.5: amount of denied boarding compensation · passengers may decline a transportation benefit and receive the cash or check payment
