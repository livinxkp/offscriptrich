money NETWORTH

five habits, one page, your number

hey, it's KP. you commented NETWORTH, so here it is. the reel gave you the five things. this is the one-page version: what each one looks like set up properly, the numbers checked against the FDIC, the IRS and the credit bureaus, and a calculator so you know your own starting number before you change anything.

who this is for

you, if you earn money but can't say what you're actually worth today, or your savings only happen when there's "something left over". skip it if you already track your net worth monthly and everything is automated. you're past this page.

your number, first

you can't improve a number you've never written down. two minutes, rough figures are fine. nothing you type leaves this page.

what am i worth today?

$—
fill in what you have and what you owe.

write the number and today's date somewhere you'll see it again. you redo this on the first of every month. the direction matters more than the size.

1. three accounts, three jobs

every dollar should have one job. most people have one account doing all three.

  • checking: bills. keep about one month of expenses here. more than that just sits.
  • high-yield savings: emergencies. three to six months of expenses, at a bank or credit union that's FDIC or NCUA insured. the point of the high-yield part: the FDIC publishes the national average savings rate every month. if your bank pays close to that average, online banks and credit unions often pay meaningfully more. compare the current APY before you move anything.
  • a roth ira: the long game. you put in money you've already paid tax on, and the growth comes out tax-free in retirement. the 2026 limit is $7,500 ($8,600 if you're 50 or older), with income limits. any major brokerage works. the full walkthrough is the ROTH guide.

2. automate it like a bill

if it depends on remembering, it won't happen in a bad month. set these up once, timed for the day after payday:

THE AUTOMATION SHEET

payday: ________

day after payday:
  checking -> hysa       $______  (until 3-6 months is full)
  checking -> roth ira   $______  (2026 max is $7,500 / 12 = $625/mo)

inside the roth:
  automatic investment into: ____________  (cash sitting in a
  roth is not invested. set the auto-buy or log in and buy.)

every credit card:
  autopay = FULL STATEMENT BALANCE, not the minimum

check-in date each month: the 1st

3. stop paying to look rich

lifestyle creep is every raise quietly turning into a bigger monthly bill. a car payment, a pricier lease, subscriptions you forgot. none of it is wrong on its own. the problem is a dollar that goes to a monthly payment is a dollar that never compounds.

the check: open last month's statement and mark every recurring charge. for each one, ask "would i sign up for this today, at this price?" cancel the noes the same day. then pick a rule for the next raise, for example half of it goes to the automation in step 2 before you ever see it.

4. protect your credit

a better score means lower rates on every loan, and the gap is thousands over a car or a mortgage. FICO weights it roughly like this: payment history 35%, amounts owed 30%, length of history 15%, new credit 10%, credit mix 10%. so the two habits that matter most are boring:

  • never miss a payment. autopay the full balance.
  • keep balances low against your limits. paying before the statement closes lowers what gets reported.

and check your reports for errors. you can pull all three bureaus free every week at annualcreditreport.com, the only site the law set up for this.

5. a budget you'll actually keep

track first, cut second. for one month, just record what comes in and what goes out, no judgement. then build the plan from what's real:

MONTHLY PLAN

in (after tax):             $______

fixed (rent, bills, debt):  $______
automated (step 2):         $______
spending, on purpose:       $______  <- the fun money. budget it
                                         so you can spend it guilt-free
buffer:                     $______

in - everything above = 0. every dollar has a job.

intentional spending, not restriction. a budget that bans everything lasts three weeks. that's Off-Script Rich.

good to know

  • net worth can go down while you're doing everything right. markets drop. that's why you track the direction over months, not the number on one day.
  • insurance limits are per depositor, per bank, per category. FDIC insurance covers up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, for each ownership category. most people never get near that. if you do, spread it.
  • the roth has rules. earned income required, income limits apply, and the earnings have a five-year and age-59-and-a-half rule. the ROTH guide has the calculator.

proof

  • FDIC: national rates and rate caps · the monthly national average savings rate to compare your bank against
  • FDIC: deposit insurance · $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category
  • IRS: 2026 IRA limit increases to $7,500 · IRA limit, catch-up, Roth income ranges
  • myFICO: what's in your credit score · the 35/30/15/10/10 weighting
