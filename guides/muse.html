which companies an ai agent can skip

hey, it's KP. you commented MUSE, so here it is. the reel was the story. this is the breakdown: what Muse actually does, what happened to the travel stocks with the real numbers, the twist most posts left out, and the five-question test i use to check whether a company i own sits in an agent's way.

who this is for

you, if you own individual stocks, or an index heavy in big tech and consumer companies, and you want a way to think about AI agents that isn't panic. skip it if you're looking for a sell list. there isn't one here, on purpose.

what muse is

Meta launched Muse on 8 September 2026: a personal AI agent for US adults, in its own app and inside WhatsApp. it can open a browser, fill out forms and handle tasks like booking travel. the part that matters for markets is that an agent like this can search, compare and book directly with an airline or a hotel, which is exactly the job aggregators get paid for.

what happened to the stocks

on Wednesday 23 September, in morning trading, 24/7 Wall St. reported Expedia down about 7%, Airbnb down 6% and Booking Holdings down 5%, while Meta rose about 2%. the fear in one sentence: if the agent books direct, the middleman's fee and the ads they sell around the search both disappear.

the twist: the day before, on 22 September, Expedia announced it was joining Muse. US travellers will be able to book hotels through the agent with Expedia staying the merchant of record, the one that takes the payment and owns the booking. no launch date was given. so the first company "being skipped" had already decided to become the plumbing underneath the thing doing the skipping.

that's the real lesson. an agent doesn't automatically delete a middleman. it changes who owns the customer, and the middlemen with the best supply can sell access to it.

the google question

the slower version of the same mechanic is search ads. ads work because a person types a question and sees ads next to the answer. if that question goes to an agent instead, the impression never happens. that's a real long-run pressure. it's also slow: Google owns the browser, the email, the maps, the video and the data that an agent would want to sit inside, and it runs its own agent in AI Mode. one bad week for travel stocks says nothing about which way that goes.

the middleman test

run any company you own through these five questions. you'll find the answers in its annual report, the 10-K, in the sections called "business" and "revenue".

THE MIDDLEMAN TEST                               yes = more exposed

1. does it mainly earn a fee for connecting a buyer
   to a seller it doesn't own?                     Y / N

2. could the seller take the order directly if a
   machine found them?                             Y / N

3. is a big share of revenue ADVERTISING sold
   around a search or a browse?                    Y / N

4. is its main edge "we're where people start
   looking", rather than supply, price or a
   product nobody else has?                        Y / N

5. has it announced deals to be the supply
   behind AI agents (Muse, AI Mode, ChatGPT)?      Y = LESS exposed

score 0-1  : agent-resistant, for now
score 2-3  : watch the next two earnings calls
score 4-5  : the market will keep repricing this on every agent headline

then the prompt to do the reading for you:

Read [company]'s most recent 10-K. Answer these five questions
with a quote and page reference for each:
1. what share of revenue is a fee for connecting buyers to sellers
   it doesn't own?
2. what share is advertising?
3. what does the company itself name as its competitive advantage?
4. does it mention AI agents or AI assistants as a risk factor?
   quote it.
5. has it announced partnerships with AI agents (Meta Muse, Google
   AI Mode, ChatGPT, Claude)?
If something isn't in the filing, say "not in the filing" instead
of guessing.

that's Off-Script Rich: read the filing before you read the comments.

good to know

  • one week is not a trend. the moves above are single-session numbers. headline-driven selling often partly reverses, and sometimes doesn't. the test above is for the next two years, not the next two days.
  • partnerships cut both ways. being the supply behind an agent keeps the booking, and probably at a thinner margin, because the agent owns the relationship. check whether margins hold in the next earnings reports.
  • ai can misread a 10-K. the prompt asks for quotes and page numbers for a reason. spot-check the two answers your decision depends on.
