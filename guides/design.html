five sites that make ai websites look expensive

hey, it's KP. you commented DESIGN, so here it is. the five sites from the reel, one for each section of a landing page, plus the part i couldn't fit: the prompt that turns a screenshot from each one into a section in your own brand instead of a generic AI layout.

who this is for

you, if you build sites with Claude, Lovable, Cursor or anything similar and the results all look like the same template. skip it if you're hiring a designer anyway. though sending them a board from these five will still save you a round of revisions.

why ai sites all look the same

AI builders default to the average of every site they've seen. same centred hero, same three feature cards, same footer. the fix isn't a better prompt in the abstract. it's giving it a specific reference for each section, so it's copying a decision someone made on purpose instead of the average.

a landing page is basically five sections: the hero, the navigation, the pricing, the call to action, and the footer. one gallery per section.

the five

1. the hero · supahero.io

the reel said superhero, it's spelled supahero. a hand-picked library of website hero sections, around 570 when i counted. scroll until one stops you. it's now part of screensdesign.com.

2. pricing · pricingpages.design

a curated gallery of real pricing pages from SaaS, ecommerce and agencies. look at how the good ones handle the middle plan. that's where most of the design decisions are.

3. navigation · navbar.gallery

navigation bars only, around 550 entries, filtered by type: sticky, dropdown, mega menu, sidebar, full-screen menu, search bar, announcement bar, breadcrumbs.

4. call to action · cta.gallery

CTA sections and buttons sorted by job: call-to-buy, download, form, pop-up, newsletter, pricing. pick by what you want the visitor to do, not by what looks nice.

5. footer · footer.design

the section everyone forgets. filter by style: typographic, large type, grid, cards, illustrative, dark, animated.

the moodboard step

before you prompt anything, take one screenshot from each of the five, all in the same mood. if your hero is quiet and editorial, don't pair it with a loud neon pricing table. five screenshots that agree with each other is the whole trick. put them in one folder named reference.

the prompts

attach the screenshots, then send this. it works in Claude, Lovable, v0, Cursor or Claude Code.

I'm building a landing page for [what you sell, one sentence].
Attached are five reference screenshots, one per section:
hero, navigation, pricing, call to action, footer.

For each section, match the LAYOUT and the DESIGN DECISIONS of its
reference: spacing, type scale, alignment, how much empty space,
how the hierarchy reads. Do not copy their words, logos, images
or brand colours.

Use my brand instead:
- colours: [hex codes]
- fonts: [names, or "pick one open-source pair that suits this"]
- tone of copy: [three words]

Mobile first, works at 360px wide with no sideways scroll.
Before you build, list the five decisions you took from each
reference so I can correct any you misread.

then tighten one section at a time.

Compare the hero you built with the hero reference. List every
difference in spacing, type size and alignment, biggest first.
Fix the top three. Change nothing else on the page.
Write the copy for this page in my voice: [paste two posts or
captions you wrote]. Keep headlines under eight words. The CTA
button says what happens next, not "submit" or "learn more".

that's Off-Script Rich: expensive is just a hundred small decisions made on purpose.

four more if you want them

  • land-book.com for whole landing pages by industry.
  • lapa.ninja for landing pages with lots of filters.
  • mobbin.com for app screens and flows. it sponsors navbar.gallery and has a paid tier.

good to know

  • inspiration, not a copy. match the layout system and the spacing. never lift their copy, images, logos or illustrations. that's both the legal line and the line where it stops looking like you.
  • check the fonts. if a reference uses a licensed font, ask for the closest open-source alternative by name. the feel shifts a little and that's fine.
  • these are free to browse. none of the five asked me to pay or sign up to look around when i checked. some have sponsors and submission forms, which is how they stay free.
